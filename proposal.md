# Policy Proposal: Managing AI-Driven Disruption of Entry-Level Knowledge Work in Australia

**To:** Deputy Secretary, Employment and Skills, Australian Government Department of Employment and Workplace Relations  
**From:** Policy Analyst  
**Date:** 14th May 2026  

## Executive Summary

Generative artificial intelligence (Gen AI) is beginning to reshape Australia’s labour market by changing the tasks that sit inside jobs, especially in entry-level knowledge work. Junior roles in administration, customer service, accounting, marketing, legal support and software support often include routine writing, summarising, data handling and communication tasks that Gen AI can now perform or assist with. The main policy challenge is not mass unemployment today, but the risk that early-career workers lose the basic tasks through which they normally build skills and progress. This proposal recommends a three-part policy package: targeted AI transition training, temporary wage and mobility support, and responsible AI adoption grants for small and medium enterprises (SMEs). This package would protect vulnerable workers while still allowing Australia to capture productivity gains from AI.

## Problem Definition and Context

Gen AI is changing work mainly by changing tasks, not simply by removing whole occupations. For example, a junior analyst may still have the same job title, but AI may now draft first versions of reports, summarise documents, prepare spreadsheets and generate client emails. Acemoglu and Restrepo (2019) explain that automation changes the “task content” of production by shifting some tasks from labour to capital, creating both displacement effects and productivity effects. This means the key policy question is not whether AI will “take all jobs”, but which tasks will be automated, which workers will be complemented, and whether new tasks are created quickly enough for labour to remain valuable.
