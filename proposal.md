# Policy Proposal: Managing AI-Driven Disruption of Entry-Level Knowledge Work in Australia

**To:** Deputy Secretary, Employment and Skills, Australian Government Department of Employment and Workplace Relations  
**From:** Policy Analyst  
**Date:** 14th May 2026  
## Executive Summary

Automating repetitive cognitive tasks, especially for entry-level knowledge workers, will impact the Australian labour market through the use of Gen AI technology to revolutionise jobs in administration, customer service, and junior analyst positions with AI having the ability to author, summarise, and process data. According to Acemoglu and Restrepo (2019), automation changes the mix of tasks in occupations leading to increased productivity as well as displacement pressures. The key policy issue facing today is not the rising rate of mass unemployment but rather the decline of early career routes for learning. Therefore, in order to create continued growth in productivity while protecting those workers who are at risk of being displaced, the recommended approach is to create a combined policy package that consists of grants to small-and-medium-sized enterprises that use AI in their operations; temporary wage assistance to affected workers; and training on how to transition to using AI in his/her job.

## Problem Definition and Context
Currently, Gen AI will change what tasks people do at work but has not yet led to other areas of the economic spectrum, such as decreasing available job opportunities. Specifically, new employees have increased vulnerability because the majority of entry-level jobs involve monotonous work that will likely become automatable due to Gen AI performing repetitive, structured tasks. Therefore, many of the administrative assistance positions (i.e., customer service representatives and clerical workers) are among those roles subject to disruption through the use of Gen AI (as stated by Jobs and Skills Australia; 2025). As many entry-level workers develop required competencies and expertise in their respective fields through performing monotonous, routine job tasks, it is important to note that eroding of these pathways may create difficulties for long-term career advancement as well as overall development within the respective workforce.

## Analysis of Labour Market Impacts
The primary impact of Gen AI upon the labour market will be the transformation of tasks. Task transformations will be based on findings provided by the International Labour Organisation (ILO; 2025), which revealed that the office administrative and clerical occupations are highly susceptible to the use of Gen AI since many cognitive repetitive tasks will likely be automated or assisted. Most job tasks still require people to provide judgement and oversight, "Thus, rather than replacing these jobs, it is very likely that most jobs will be redesigned". Based on this, the movement towards policy development focused on providing unemployment protection for displaced workers should shift towards policy initiatives focused on adapting to the workforce changes brought by the advent of Gen AI. 
Asymmetrically productivity growth exists in terms of AI use for both employees and businesses. According to research done by Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond (2025), AI tools improve worker’s productivity in customer-related roles, especially for those with limited skill sets. However, these improvements can only be seen by those who have had adequate training and created a work environment supporting this type of productivity. Without investing complementary to the capabilities of the workforce, it is likely the increases in productivity will only be seen in high-skilled employees at larger companies.

Another negative outcome associated with the implementation of AI into the workplace is the increasing inequality experienced by businesses/organisations of all sizes and labour force participants (workers). As a general rule, larger companies have more financial and technical means to effectively adopt AI than SMEs and those in the labour force who do not have the necessary digital access or experience. Jobs and Skills Australia (2025) highlights that those in the workforce with digital access and/or limited ability to access skills training will experience greater disruption than individuals who have either. Therefore, equity issues should be considered a priority in determining how the transition to AI occurs.

## Policy Options and Evaluation

A National AI Transition Training Programme, which can be offered by universities or TAFE (Technical and Further Education), is the preferred choice for workers in entry-level knowledge positions. Staff members will be taught about AI literacy, how to use AI in conjunction with their duties, and how digital technology works through short courses, or short practical classes. These training sessions are intended to be short-term training for employees to adjust to their ever-expanding job roles and to increase an organisation’s future performance. The implementation of this type of program will require a significant financial investment, as well as considerable coordination between educational institutions and the government.

Relocation assistance and wage insurance are another form of assistance for workers displaced by AI. They will give workers temporary income support while they learn new skills or take new jobs. By reducing the financial stress on a worker, these two types of assistance will also help workers transition to their new employment situation. However, these two types of assistance will not assist workers in developing skills for future jobs unless it is provided in conjunction with retraining programs.

Small-to-Medium Enterprise (SME) responsible Artificial Intelligence grants are a third option to promote the use of AI systems by businesses with human oversight and employee training as criteria for implementation. This option could increase production and help small business remain competitive. Public funds would require oversight to ensure they are used efficiently.
## Recommended Policy Package and Implementation Plan

It is recommended that a combined AI Labour Transition Package is provided, consisting of Training support, Wage support and Responsible SME AI Adoption Grants.   This is a far better alternative to having one single policy in place as workers will require new skills to continue with their employment, and business will require support in adopting the AI technology, and protection for displaced workers will be required on a short-term basis. The combined package approach would assist Australia not only improve their productivity but also decrease inequality and protecting entry-level career pathways.

Implementation should begin by identifying occupations where entry-level tasks are highly exposed to Gen AI, particularly in administrative, customer-service and junior analytical roles. Jobs and Skills Australia (2025) argues that a targeted approach is important because the effects of AI will not be experienced equally across industries or worker groups. Early pilot programs should focus on AI literacy, digital communication and human-AI collaboration through TAFE and university partnerships.

The government should also provide temporary wage and mobility support for workers affected by AI disruption while expanding SME grants linked to worker training and human oversight. The Productivity Commission (2024) states that AI productivity gains are strongest when businesses combine technology adoption with workforce capability development. This means support should focus not only on purchasing AI tools, but also on helping workers adapt to changing tasks.

Success should be measured through outcomes such as course completion, employment retention and wage recovery rather than enrolment numbers alone. A practical evaluation framework would help ensure public funding supports real labour-market adjustment and long-term workforce resilience.

## Risks and Mitigations
Training programmes run the risk of becoming too generic and divorced from the demands of the workplace. Regular curriculum revisions and business partnerships can help to reduce this. Another risk is that companies use AI funding more to cut labour costs than to support workers. One way to address this problem would be to require human monitoring and workforce training as a condition of financing. There’s also the issue of unequal access so support should be targeted at low income and regional workers.

 ## Conclusion
 Gen AI is reshaping labour markets by automating some tasks while increasing productivity in others. Australia’s challenge is to manage this transition fairly while still supporting innovation and growth. A combined package of AI training, wage support and responsible SME adoption grants would help workers adapt, strengthen entry-level career pathways and ensure productivity gains are shared more broadly.
